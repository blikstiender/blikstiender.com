# You Probably Don't Even Hear It When It Happens
*January 29, 2026*

[Dan Mcquade](https://defector.com/dan-mcquade-1983-2026) has died. May his memory be a blessing.  

___

On an off-hand recommendation I recently read Didion's *Year of Magical Thinking*. It's about grief, and watching death, but there's very little about the death itself. 

___

I was having dinner with my dad and his surgeon friend about a year ago. The surgeon is nearing retirement age, and we were talking about his death. He alluded to the death of Daniel Khaneman. A party, followed by a trip to the assisted dying facility (is that even the right verbiage?) A goodbye, and then some. 

A friend of my dad's did the same not too long ago. Her ALS had progressed rapidly over the course of a mere 6 months. It was time.

My dad didn't mention how he wanted to die. He just wanted to avoid the whole dying part. His POLST is restrictive and clear. No tubes, no nothing. He made sure that I knew where one of the copies was, and that my mom had a picture of it on her phone. He wondered if I wanted him to send me a picture of the POLST. I said I was fine with just knowing where it is in the cabinet in the living room.

The conversation moved on to the topic of "seeing" death. My dad is a high-acuity anesthethiologist. He sees people die. The surgeon mostly does traumas. GSWs (Gun Shot Wound) and MVAs (Motor Vehicle Accidents, or pithily in the EMS community: "Man vs. Auto"). He also sees people. Very often they've both seen people die together. This seemed mundane, even procedural to both of them. The first word that comes to mind is "desensitized." But the connotation there is not right. It's not that they don't care, I know. Rather: there's a learned acceptance. When death comes it tends to stay. Warding it off is feasible, but not preventible. The moment itself, is unremarkable. 

___

Samantha also died recently. May her memory be a blessing. I found out via a youtube video her husband posted. 

___

I try to find art about it. And this is still my favorite thing made about death. 

Mourning is a complex and stochastic process. Debilitating grief hits like waves. Suddenly, triggered by the very mundane. 

But death itself. As it occurs. I can't find any poetry in the moment itself. It just happens. One moment they are are - and then next
