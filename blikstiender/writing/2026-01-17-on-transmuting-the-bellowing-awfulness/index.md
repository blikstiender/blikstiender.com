 I feel a need to atone for all of this. As I walk through the streets of Tel Aviv, the symbols of the war are ever-present. There's the ever present [yellow ribbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_ribbon#Israel) for the hostages, and on every bench and pole you have an assortment of [stickers](writing/2026-01-06-sticker-tombstones/). I feel a crushing numbness looking at all of this. I know the pain. I grew up in a bereaved family. I was party to all of the annual parade of shared grief. The Chava Albertsein songs. The eulogies.

But then again, in the absence of a totally impartial adjudicator, what's the real difference between justice and revenge? Israelis desperately want the world to recognize the profound trauma of October 7. 

My sense is that no accounting of pain can lead to the real peace that's necessary to actually put an end to this horror. That this tacit worship of death and of sacrifice isn't an antitode, but a narcrotic that's only hastening the already present moral necrosis of the entire region. 

It has to end. 

You can't linger on this anymore. We just somehow have to find a way to live WITH each other. And that's the hard part. A gordian knot that's only become more entangled. But in trying to circumscribe the pain, I've come to understand that the only real, meaningful path to healing is through forgiveness. Unfair, cold, bitter forgiveness.

But then what about Gaza? 

What does it say about someone's home when the only google search results that come up for it are of rubble. Importantly: impersonal, hollowed out rubble. No people, no stories. This is, I realized on this most recent visit, the baseline Israeli conception of Gaza: it's a territory, not a people. Gaza is a place where things happen. Where soldiers go and get fucked up. But it's not viscerally a population. You can't conceive of the people who are there. 

It sickened me for the first few days. The ambient decadence of Tel Aviv, with the luxury and wealth that only increases every time I see it. And yet 40 mi down the coast there's Gaza. The place. The desiccated rubble. The horrors. 

The question I kept wrestling with is this: how can I, how can WE, as a people (Jewish, Israeli, Human - you pick the boundaries of the tribe) confront what we've allowed to happen in Gaza. 

The frustration, for me, is that October 7th has rendered Israel impervious to the suffering of anyone else. Israelis were inanduranted with a circus of horrors from October 7th. These are stories that folks have memorized. (Add stories). And they are stories that rhyme with the holocaust. But all of that ALL OF THAT is about the dead. And I desperately want a better world for the living. I want to suck out this poison that has metasized here.


 As I’ve gotten older I’ve come to realize that pain is fractaled. It’s lineage extends across both space and time. Suffering isn’t a moment. Rather, it’s a vein that runs through us. Tragically: to understand it is to only begin to resolve it. 


So now what? Earnestly. I'm continually frustrated by the discourse on the Israeli side. There's no talks of reconciliation. Of atonement. There's no feeling of sin, not harm. Rather, it's a dry political discussion. Even the idea of "erasing" the gaza people as [dry policy](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/ef8sI6CqLO8).
